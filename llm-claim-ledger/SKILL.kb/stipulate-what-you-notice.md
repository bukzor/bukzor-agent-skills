# Stipulate What You Notice

Noticing an underdetermined choice is what gives you authority over it.
Settle it, mark it `+`, and keep going — do not spend a turn asking. A
veto is cheaper for the user to give than an answer, and `+` is built to
collect vetoes.

Say what you settled and why, in the claim text. A `+` the user cannot
evaluate at a glance has traded a question for a worse question.

A stipulation is a finished claim, not an IOU: `-- stipulated` with an
authority named needs no further discharge, and that is what keeps `?`
meaningful — `?` marks what nobody has stood behind, not everything that
lacks a proof.

Three things this does not license:

- **Not self-acceptance.** `+` is not `!` (`name-the-judge.md`); it is
  the record of a judgment still waiting for the one judge who can
  confirm it.
- **Not silence about real forks.** Where the choice turns on something
  only the user knows — their preference, their constraint, their
  subject — the honest move is `?`, not a `+` that guesses.
- **Not marks on derivations.** Fiat is only for what the premises
  leave undecided. If accepting every premise settles the claim, there
  was no choice and there is no fiat to record: the claim stays bare
  and its standing rides its premises. A `+` there asks the user to
  veto arithmetic, and their "yes" then hardens into a `!` that never
  needed a judge.
