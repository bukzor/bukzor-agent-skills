# Authority Belongs to Acts

An act has an author, and authority is a property of acts rather than of
propositions. Nothing is authoritative; someone authored something.

So a recorded judgment that omits its judge is not a weaker record of
the same thing — it is a different kind of thing, a hidden oracle. It
reports that a question was settled while concealing what would unsettle
it, and every later reader inherits the settlement without inheriting
the means to review it.

Hidden oracles are the failure a ledger exists to prevent, which is why
the recording of a judgment and the naming of its judge are one act, not
two.
