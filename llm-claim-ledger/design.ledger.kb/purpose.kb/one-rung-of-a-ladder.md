# One Rung of a Ladder

This is the semi-formal rung: more than prose, less than a proof
assistant. Below it, a handful of lines pasted into a chat client with
no files and no tools. Above it, typed claim kernels and proof-transport
systems that enforce what this rung only records.

The rungs are meant to interoperate, and that constrains the design
here in two directions. Downward: the notation must degrade to the rung
below without losing claims, so the smallest deployment stays legal in
the largest (bare-form-stays-legal.md). Upward: it must not commit to
semantics a stricter rung would have to undo — which is why premise
arrows stay informal rather than half-formal
(../notation.kb/arrows-are-informal.md).

The temptation this resists is climbing. Each rung is a genuine
equilibrium of rigour against cost, and a semi-formal notation that
grows checkable inference has not become the rung above — it has left
its own.
