# An Early Stub Expansion Costs the Eager Listing

Expanding a stub before its occasion begins -- out of curiosity, or a
turn-one sweep -- costs exactly what listing the whole bank eagerly
costs today, and nothing more. Omitting stubs from the listing breaks
recognition: an agent cannot notice a set it has never seen named. So
stubs are shown, and early expansion is a bounded cost, not a failure.
If a stub is expanded every session, that is evidence the occasion is
near top and the set should be flattened back, not a delivery defect.
