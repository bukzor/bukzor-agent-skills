# Confinement

An `ontology:` excludes: a word listed there is that theory's, and its
siblings may not use it. Descendants need no permission -- they read
every word their container coins -- and another ledger is out of
jurisdiction. So the scan is not "a word off the list"; it is "a
sibling saying an owned word", and the finding is one of five.

- The word was never the owner's to take -- topic vocabulary rather
  than a coinage, however central to the subject. Cull it.
- The claim is misfiled. Move it under the owner.
- The speaker genuinely depends on the owner. Admit it: import the
  owner's theory. Priced: under the fused `why:` an import asserts
  support as well as vocabulary, so take it only where "my theory
  depends on theirs" is true in both senses.
- Both mean it, differently. One of them uniquifies -- usually by
  restoring the phrase its own prose already uses, since a list entry
  truncated to its head noun is how an ownable term becomes unownable.
- Both mean it the same way, and the speaker is one of the owner's own
  priors. Then the word is owned too far downstream: it wants a theory
  upstream of everyone who needs it, which they import. A word every
  sibling says is not an unownable word -- it is a missing shared
  prior, and this is the reading to reach for before culling anything
  the ledger genuinely stipulates.

Silence is not health: an ontology nobody has narrowed in a while may
just mean nobody scanned.
