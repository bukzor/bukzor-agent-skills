# Devlog: 2026-08-20 — Centrality is not coinage: three rulings overturned

## Focus

The user overturned this morning's Decisions 2–4 (commit 0e18b77):
'ontology', 'retraction', and 'transport' are plain English too, and
each needed the PLAIN menu — removed or elaborated/contracted — not a
winner picked among stipulators.

## What went wrong, named

Asked directly whether the documented decision process was used: not
fully. DOCKET's menu was used, but the classification step — plain
English vs coinage — ran on an undocumented agent test: how central
the word is to the coining theory. The user's ruling supplies the
right axis: **whether a theory uses a word is poorly correlated with
whether it is violating encapsulation / separation of concerns / the
owning theory's primacy** — a word plenty of unrelated theories will
need must not be owned, however invested the coining theory is.
Centrality measures the owner's investment; the law cares about other
theories' need. 'ontology' is maximally central to notation and still
ordinary philosophical English; 'transport' is standard mathematics
(the same ground fixpoint's 'product' was culled on); 'retraction' is
everyday English whose technical form history has not even specified
(its RETRACTION claim is open — nothing coined, so nothing ownable
yet).
