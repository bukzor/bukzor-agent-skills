# Devlog: 2026-08-20 — A ledger root cannot prosecute, and that is what it is for

## Focus

A peer session read devlog 011's derivation — a root stipulation can
never generate a finding — and proposed acting on it: **a ledger root
cannot own**, its `ontology:` is a reading aid, and the words move back
down to the theories that coin them. Rejected, on three grounds. The
argument was worth the hour: it found one real stale sentence, and the
thing that stopped it being written again is now in `OUTERMOST_WINS`.

## The argument

Two rules compose badly if you only read them. Ownership fires on the
owner's siblings and nowhere else (`REACH`). A ledger root has no
siblings, so its entry can never charge anyone. And `OUTERMOST_WINS`
says that where root and descendant both list a word, the *root* owns.
So every root/descendant double resolves toward the one site where
ownership provably cannot fire — and `97031cb` culled the descendant
copies of `stratum`, `tower`, `extension`, `enforcement`, leaving four
words with no live owner anywhere.

## The inner copy was never the enforceable one

"Inert" is what `OUTERMOST_WINS` had already made those entries — the
scan said so, in those words, before the cull. `--census` read
`0 trespasses over 0 stipulations` on both sides of the commit.
Enforcement did not fall from real to zero; it was zero either way, and
the cull removed four lines that were already doing nothing. The
composition is a real property of the law. It is not a regression, and
it does not date from this week.

## Outside `tower.kb`, `stratum` is the house format

`SHOULD_OWN` asks whether the word turning up elsewhere would be
evidence of a concerns violation. The proposal asserted that
`record.kb` or `history.kb` saying `stratum` is exactly the level
confusion the tower theory exists to name. Every such use in the
ledger:

    data-representation:  The seam stratum: ...
    data-structures:      The inventory stratum: ...
    genre:                The theory-of-theories stratum: ...
    history:              The substrate stratum: ...
    protocol:             The enforcement stratum: ...
    record:               The typing stratum: ...
    reference:            The graph stratum: ...
    standing:             The epistemic stratum: ...

One sentence, eight times: a theory naming its own floor, in the
ledger's format for the orientation blurb. Owning `stratum` at
`tower.md` fires on eight self-descriptions — `SHOULD_OWN`'s third goal
by name, a license that cries wolf.

There is no repair available either. `TOWER` sits above those theories,
so the findings land *below* the owner, where importing is a cycle. The
direction law's remedies there are reword, move the claim up, or **move
the word down**. Moving `stratum` down to the root is the prescribed
repair, already applied. The arrangement the proposal read as a leak is
the law working.

## The cost is twenty-one entries, not two

The proposal priced itself at `ENGINE`'s `regime` and `economics`. But
"a root cannot own" also voids `llm-claims/design.claims.md`, which
owns twenty-one words — `ledger`, `claim`, `theory`, `prior`, `label`,
`sigil`, `standing` — promoted there one turn earlier precisely to
clear twenty-two trespass findings. Push them back down and all
twenty-two return, every one a false positive, because every theory in
a claims ledger talks about claims. Root promotion is the law's main
repair instrument. The proposal removes it from the toolbox.

The named cheaper alternative — a carve-out in `OUTERMOST_WINS` —
was rejected with it. There is no symptom to patch.

## What a root listing actually does

The premise "the root's entry does nothing" is the part that was
missing, not wrong. A root entry cannot prosecute, true — but it makes
any descendant's listing of that word inert. That is the ledger
declaring the word **common property: no theory here may fence it.**
For `stratum`, `claim`, `ledger`, that is the correct statement, and it
is the negative half of ownership doing exactly the job those words
need.

Nothing in the corpus said so, which is why three sessions have now
derived it independently. `OUTERMOST_WINS` says it now.
