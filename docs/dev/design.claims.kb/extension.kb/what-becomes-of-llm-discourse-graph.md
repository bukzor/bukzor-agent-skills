# What Becomes of llm-discourse-graph?

**It is reformed in place as an extension of `/llm-claims`**, keeping
its name, its location, and its instances. Outmoded as a five-type
format, not as a skill. The other two candidates in
`what-becomes-of-llm-discourse-graph.kb/` were rejected and stay
there as the record of roads not taken.

## Where the stored-likelihood argument belongs

A stored number the reader did not watch you derive is lawful in
exactly two ways:

- **cache** -- computed, and it says what from (*stamped*: which
  revision of its inputs) and can be recomputed (*regenerable*: the
  evaluator still exists and runs). A reader can tell whether it has
  gone stale, and refresh it.
- **testimony** -- judged rather than computed, and it says whose
  judgment (*attributed*) and when (*dated*). A reader cannot refresh
  it, but can weigh it: an estimate someone made in March is a
  different thing from a fact.

A number that is neither can be neither refreshed nor weighed.
`likelihood` sits there, since attribution and date are optional
fields and the skill ships no evaluator.

That is evidence the format has fallen behind, and none at all for
deleting anything: the gap closes cheaply, because git supplies the
missing attribution and date. It bears on `migration-plan.md` (carry
the values over, supplying the two fields) and on
`what-need-does-a-stored-likelihood-serve.md` (whether a scalar is
the right shape for a hedge at all), not on this question.
