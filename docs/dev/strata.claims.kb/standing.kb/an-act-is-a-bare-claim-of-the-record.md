# An Act Is a Bare Claim of the Record

Every judgment -- a fiat, a ruling, a certificate, a retraction, a
verdict -- is one act: an assessor, a target, a verdict, an
occasion. An act's record is a historical proposition, checkable
against the record itself, so it enters the base as a bare claim --
one sort, a structured species -- and the base stays claims-only
with the evidence inside it.

- A target is a claim, including another act's record. The occasion
  ordering makes act-on-act reference well-founded: a target must
  precede the act that cites it.
- An act-record is never false once true. Contravention strikes an
  act's force (force-is-computed-never-asserted.md); a
  truth-contest disputes whether the act happened; the two never
  merge.
- A claim is not its authorship event: a claim's identity survives
  restatement, while an act is indexical and unrepeatable.

The declined alternative -- acts as a second sort of entry -- would
fire this theory's own staleness condition and buys nothing a
species does not.
