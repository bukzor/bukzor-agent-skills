# The Deflation Is One-Way

Acts reduce to claims at the price of one law -- force. Claims do
not reduce to acts: a fold of acts into claims must supply a
content-equivalence, and every candidate fails.

- Textual identity breaks a claim's survival of restatement.
- Semantic identity puts node-identity downstream of the rulings
  that settle sameness, merger, and supersession -- the quotient
  presupposes acts of identification aimed at named loci, which are
  claims renamed.
- Declared loci are claims outright.

Genetically every claim arrives by an act of authorship;
structurally its identity outruns every such event. A reduction
whose residue is one law is a deflation; one whose residue is an
unbounded set of future rulings is a relocation.
