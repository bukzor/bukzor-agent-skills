# Higher Sorts Are Definitional

Higher theories introduce real sorts at their level of abstraction
-- a question, an objection, a procedure -- and each is admitted by
its down-translation: underneath, it is base-sort entries plus
vocabulary and laws confined to the new sorts. The translation is
the admission ticket, and it buys the metatheorem: extensions by
defined sorts with confined rules are conservative, proven once for
the framework and inherited by every genre. Only a primitive sort --
no translation, rules reaching old entries -- reopens a per-genre
proof.

Sort-count may also shrink upward: a higher theory may define a
quotient that collapses lower distinctions, a forgetting view. What
no extension may do is delete or redefine a lower sort -- reduction
as view, never as revision.

This is how a typed surface avoids being a silo: own vocabulary and
laws above, shared substrate and tooling through the translation
below.
