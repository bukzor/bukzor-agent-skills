# Assessor Identity Carries No Force of Its Own

To a reader who admits every act, who judged is invisible: rename
the assessors however you like -- three names into one, or one into
three -- and every claim keeps its disposition. Authority reaches the
computation through that reader's stance, and through nothing else.

So the algebra offers no seniority, no tie-break by name, no house
assessor:

- a clash computes to the contested interval whether the clashing
  assessors are two or one. An assessor is not assumed
  self-consistent, and one who contradicts themself contests their
  own claim exactly as a stranger would
  (contravention-must-name-its-target.md).
- identity is read in one other place only -- an assessor may annul
  their own act, and every stance admits that -- and the rule is
  invisible to the reader who admits everything, for whom the strike
  counted anyway.

Under a partial stance the invariance genuinely fails: merging two
assessors into one can turn a strike that reader discounted into a
self-annulment they must admit. That is the claim's content and not
a hole in it -- names matter exactly as far as some reader's stance
makes them matter, and never on their own.

The declined alternative is standing that reads identity directly: a
seniority order, an owner, a default arbiter. At unbounded scale
there is no global arbiter even in principle (STANCE), so any such
order is one reader's stance smuggled into the law, where nobody can
decline it.

Standing is `bare` on that reading of the fold; `verify:` quantifies
the invariance over generated records and renamings, bounded
evidence (`../../strata.claims.md`, Verify) rather than the proof.
