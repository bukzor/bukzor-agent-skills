# Settle a Conflict by Re-derivation

Two contenders for one place -- two names, two forms, incumbent text
against a newcomer -- are settled by re-deriving the answer from the
governing law at the consumer's position. Seniority, arrival order,
and authorship are addresses, not grounds: keeping the incumbent on
those grounds takes recorded justification, and is how a defect stays
canonical.

- The record's algebra already refuses them one level down: assessor
  identity carries no force (BLIND) and recency resolves nothing
  (EXPLICIT). Those laws govern the standing of acts; this one
  carries the refusal to design decisions, which the algebra does not
  reach.
- Re-derived, the senior side may simply lose. In the ruling above
  every criterion inverted the answer: the elder label had been
  misnamed since it was minted, the collision merely exposed it, and
  the switching cost the recommendation leaned on was never
  load-bearing
  (`../../../devlog/2026-08-31-000-The-rule-was-already-written--what-was-missing-was-its-arrival.md`).
- Where one variant has proven to work and the broader incumbent has
  not, the proven one becomes canonical
  (`../../../adr/2026-08-29-001-No-aliasing--one-body-gets-one-filename.md`).

REFORM is the neighbouring preference and not this one: it prefers
one artifact over two, and says nothing about which of two contenders
survives. Declined: the tiebreak by arrival order and re-signing
cost, which was the recommendation the correction reversed.
