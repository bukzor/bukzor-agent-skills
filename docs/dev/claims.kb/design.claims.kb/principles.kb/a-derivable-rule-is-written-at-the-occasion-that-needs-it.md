# A Derivable Rule Is Written at the Occasion That Needs It

Logic says what follows from the record; an occasion says what a reader needs
where they stand. A consequence is written down when some occasion needs it and
the reader there will not reliably derive it: the premises sit far from the
occasion or on separate branches, or a miss there is costly. It is written at
that occasion, where the reader already is, as a trigger names its condition:
a corollary citing its premises in `why:`, which stay the home of its reasoning
(`ONE_HOME`), and naming the occasion that earned it. A miss in practice is the
strongest evidence of such an occasion; a foreseeable one needs no miss first.
