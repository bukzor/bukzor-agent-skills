# Directives name the act

An agent-facing directive names the act to perform. A prohibition
bounds the space of acts without locating one in it, and the reader
has to infer the act it was meant to leave standing — inference under
instruction being where drift enters. Ten prohibitions still leave the
act undetermined; one statement of the act determines it and makes the
prohibitions redundant.

The failure is invisible to the author, because the author already
knows the act. They write the boundary they just watched someone
cross, read it back, and see the whole rule — their own knowledge
supplying the part the text omits.

Two consequences for how the text grows:

- **It scales with the task, not with the failure log.** A
  prohibition list accretes a line per incident and never subtracts,
  because no single line is ever wrong. Naming the act absorbs
  incidents instead of appending to them.
- **The test is executability.** Can a reader act from this sentence
  alone, without reconstructing the situation that prompted it? If
  the sentence only rules something out, it has not yet been written.

The declined alternative is the prohibition list, and it is tempting
for the same reason a procedure is: every line was once a real
failure. That is the tell, not the warrant — a rule earns its form
from what the next reader can do with it, not from what the last
reader did wrong.

A negative still earns its place where the positive form cannot carry
the meaning: a genuine exclusion (`## When NOT to trigger`), a
one-way door, a boundary whose whole content is the boundary. The
claim is about directives that have an act to name, which is most of
them.

Usually shorter than what it replaces, so unlike
`stance-over-procedure.md` it does not trade against
`price-text-by-load-frequency.md`.

## Warrant

The owner's ruling, 2026-09-01, on a draft bloat-audit rule that
listed what an author should leave out: "I like the intent but it
seems wordy and negative. Instead, crystallize what *should* be done,
then ensure the directions match that. I don't want paragraphs listing
all kinds of things that the agent shouldn't do. In short, be
suspicious of writing 'not' assertions for agent contexts."

Generalizing that from one bloat-audit rule to every agent-facing
directive in the fleet is the agent's step, hence `standing: agent`.
