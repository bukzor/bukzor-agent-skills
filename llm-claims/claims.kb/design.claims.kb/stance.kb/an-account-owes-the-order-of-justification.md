# An Account Owes the Order of Justification

An account is owed in the order that justifies it, not the order in
which it was found. Discovery order is contingent on who searched, in
what mood, and what they happened to try first. Justification order is a
property of the claims themselves. Rewriting the first into the second
asserts nothing new and retracts nothing, so it is no falsification: an
account's content is its commitments and their support, and neither
moves when the sequence does.

What the rewrite costs is the search path, and with it the evidence of
what was already attempted. So the test is never whether a passage is
historical, but whether it is doing justificatory work:

- an alternative eliminated, so it is not tried again;
- a stipulation's provenance, so the act behind it can be appealed to;
- the condition under which the account expires.

Those are the account. The remainder is scaffolding, and an author who
leaves it standing has mistaken the record of the building for the
building.

Declined: keeping discovery order on the ground that it is what actually
happened. It is. The act being justified is the one in force now, and an
author answerable for a stopping point owes the grounds, not the
itinerary.
