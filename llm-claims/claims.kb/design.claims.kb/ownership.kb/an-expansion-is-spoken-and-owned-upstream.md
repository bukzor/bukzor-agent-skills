# An expansion is spoken, and owned upstream

PLAIN's second move -- expand the entry to the term of art the theory
coins -- carries two conditions its worked instance never had to
state, because there the phrase was already in the prose and the two
senses were genuinely different:

- **A minted phrase is not owned until the prose says it.**
  `ontology:` is evidential, so an entry naming a phrase no claim
  speaks records a coinage that never happened. Where the prose
  already carries the phrase, expanding is bookkeeping; where the
  phrase is minted to cover the uses, editing every site to say it is
  part of the repair rather than follow-up work.
- **The surviving entry goes to the stipulator the others import.**
  Contending stipulators are siblings, and REACH bars a sibling that
  does not import. Expand at the importer and the imported party is
  left saying a word outside its reach -- a contention traded for a
  trespass. Expand at the imported party and the arrow already there
  licenses the rest.

Where the two senses agree, the double says the word is too coarse for
both rather than wrong in one. Then one phrase covers every use, and
the repair is a uniquify at the owner with a cull at each other
stipulator -- a compound, priced as both. Which stipulator keeps the
entry is not settled by counting uses: concentration measures who
leans on the word, licensing runs along `why:` arrows, and only the
arrows say which choice leaves a trespass behind.

The declined alternative is a fresh phrase per stipulator. That is
right only where the senses differ; minting two where one serves
asserts a distinction the prose cannot show, and doubles the
vocabulary the reader carries.

Worked 2026-08-29 in a consuming ledger: one word, two stipulators,
one sense -- expanded to a two-word phrase at the imported stipulator,
culled at the importer, five prose sites edited. Contentions fell 2 to
0 with the trespass count unmoved at 62, which is the check that the
entry went to the right side.
