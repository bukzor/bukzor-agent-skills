# An idle import is adjudicated by reading

An import with neither lens witnessed -- the taker's interior never
says the taken words (LIVE, read across the arrow), and none of its
claims cites into the cited theory's tree -- cannot be ruled on
mechanically: under the fused `why:` support may still ride the
arrow itself, and only reading the citing theory tells. So the scan
files these as an adjudication queue, never as errors -- each entry
resolves by reading into keep, cull, or refile (IDLE_TEST). The
queue turns over as imports are minted and read, which is it
working, so its length is a reading and never a score.

**Contested 2026-09-03 by the author of WHY_IS_FUSED**: "A rests on B
logically, but mentions none of its claims, none of its ontology?
Sounds impossible, to me." Under that, an arrow with no lexical trace
of any kind is not support, so a true idle entry is always a repair --
cull or retarget -- and the queue is an error list after all. This
stands open rather than struck because the scan's channels are
narrower than the test: it reads stipulated words and interior `why:`,
never prose naming the prior's claim labels, which
`llm-claims-kb-mentions` now computes exactly. Today's queue therefore
holds traced imports the scan cannot see. Fuse that third channel
first; then rule this by editing `standing:`.
