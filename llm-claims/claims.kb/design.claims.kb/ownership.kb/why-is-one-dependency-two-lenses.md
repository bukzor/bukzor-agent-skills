# `why:` is one dependency, two lenses

Support and vocabulary share the one `why:` field on purpose: a
dependency claimed under either lens is checkable under both, and a
disagreement between them -- an import whose support is real but
whose words go unsaid, or the reverse -- is a factoring smell in the
citing theory, not a defect in the field. The notation's statement
that the two are independent stands as a statement of possibility;
this ruling adds the norm that in a well-factored ledger they
coincide, so each lens audits the other.

The author sharpened this 2026-09-03: a disagreement is not merely a
smell to note but a problem wanting a solution, and the two directions
are not symmetric.

- **Support with no mention** -- A rests on B logically, yet names no
  claim of B and says none of its words -- does not happen. This
  field records the claims whose collapse would send you back to this
  one, and text touching nothing of B's registers no collapse of B:
  an arrow with no lexical trace cannot be maintained, which is the
  same as not being support. An apparent instance is a reading
  failure or a scan too narrow, never a well-factored theory.
- **Mention with no support** -- A names a claim or word of B and
  rests on none of it -- does happen, and has four repairs, in the
  author's order: narrow B's ontology; move the cited claim to a
  theory A does rest on; move the mention to a theory that does rest
  on B; or drop the mention, which is rarely the right thing.

So the norm has teeth in both directions. Coincidence is not the
well-factored ledger's happy accident but its test, and either lens
failing alone names the repair.
