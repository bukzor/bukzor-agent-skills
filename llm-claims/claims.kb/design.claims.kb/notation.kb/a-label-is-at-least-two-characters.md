# A Label Is At Least Two Characters

A label is two characters or longer. The grammar is otherwise
unchanged: upper snake case, first character a letter.

The bare rendering is the mention form (LABEL_FORMATS), so every
label-shaped token in prose is a claim name until something says
otherwise. A one-character grammar makes each sentence-initial `A` and
first-person `I` a potential citation -- measured on the fleet
2026-09-01, those two tokens alone account for 159 of the 200
label-shaped non-citations in claim prose. Two characters is the floor
that keeps the bare form parseable.

The rule predates its record: specified early, it never landed in the
schema or the manual, and the tools faithfully copied the written
grammar -- the schema's label pattern admitted single characters from
its first version. Landing it costs nothing today; the shortest live
label in the fleet is three characters.
