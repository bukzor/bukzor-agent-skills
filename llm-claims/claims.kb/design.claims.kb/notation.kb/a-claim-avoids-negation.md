# A Claim Avoids Negation

Treat "not", "rather than", or any other negation in a claim's text as a
warning, not a style choice. It earns its place only where the contrast
itself carries direct, context-free utility — naming a declined
alternative the reader would otherwise reach for. Everywhere else it is
a revision scar: the line records the argument that produced it instead
of the state it reached, taxing a reader who was not in that argument.

The sharpest case is a claim about an absence — "the procedure has no
stopping rule" catalogues something missing, and a catalogue of what is
missing has no natural end. What is meant is almost always a question:
`?`, naming what an answer would settle, not a `+` or `!` naming what
is not there.
