# Before Signing a Claim

The sigil signs the judge; anything finer names the judge in a status
-- `-- certified(pytest 8.2: 041 passed)`, `-- authority: RFC 9110`.
An unattributed judgment is a hidden oracle, and hidden oracles are
the failure this notation exists to prevent.

The judge's name carries its version wherever the verdict could turn on
it: `certified(CHECK)` promises a *re-runnable* check, and a check whose
identity is "the linter" is not re-runnable.

Signing is spending. A `+` is not caution but debt: every
agent-signed claim joins the user's next review sweep, to be closed
out there -- refactored away, corrected to bare, or ruled `!`. So
first try to make the signature unnecessary: a premise set that
settles the claim, or a check that certifies it, closes it free.
Sign `+` only where judgment genuinely remains. A `!` is the
opposite motion -- the user's judgment is already spent, and
recording it is what makes it reusable.

Never accept your own output. `claim accept` is the user's call --
sign your own judgments `+` and let them stand until the user rules
(`../when/you-notice-an-underdetermined-choice.md`).
