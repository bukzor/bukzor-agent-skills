# Prefer Fewer Distinctions

A distinction earns its place by what it lets us state, not by what it
lets us tell apart. The world admits endless distinctions; the ones
worth adopting are the ones that make some judgment recordable which
otherwise could not be recorded at all.

The costs and the benefits fall in different places. An added
distinction is paid for by every author, at every act, in the moment
before each one when they must decide which side of it they are on. It pays back only where it is load-bearing.

So the test runs on the benefit side: name the judgment that becomes
sayable. "It would be tidier" and "they really are different" are true
of every distinction ever proposed, and so decide nothing.
