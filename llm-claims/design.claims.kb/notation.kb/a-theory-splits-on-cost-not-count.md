# A Theory Splits on Cost, Not Count

Two triggers license a new theory, and both price the reader's work.
When more than one theory leans on the same claims of a third, those
claims come out as the shared prior of both -- left inline, each
leaning theory re-derives them and the copies drift. And a split
earns its boundary when most parts come out easier to read: shorter
ontology, shorter arrows, claims arguing in one vocabulary instead of
two. The new boundary is a distinction, and pays rent the way any
distinction does (../stance.kb/prefer-the-smaller-ontology.md).

The declined trigger is length: a theory never splits because its
claim list got long. Count is a proxy for the reader's cost, which
the real triggers name directly, and a split made on count draws a
boundary with no vocabulary behind it -- the word-list rule then has
nothing to police, so the boundary is decoration.
