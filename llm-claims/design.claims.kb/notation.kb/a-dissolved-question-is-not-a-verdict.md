# ~~A Dissolved Question Is Not a Verdict~~ -- retracted, redone as WITHDRAWN

The category-error diagnosis (dissolved judges the framing, not the
content) survives into `a-verdict-names-what-the-judge-ruled.md` --
but as a distinction the
*word* carries within one field, not as a second field the *schema*
carries. This file's mistake was proposing a boolean before checking
whether `../purpose.kb/every-claim-sound-open-or-retracted.md` even
asks for one; it does not, and reasoning from it directly gives the
smaller answer.

A defeated claim (`defeated: true`) is judged on its content and
loses. A dissolved one is never reached: its presupposition fails
first, so there is no content left to judge true or false. That is a
verdict on the claim's framing, not on what it asserted -- a different
question than VOID answers, and putting both on one mark would be the
same category error `strata.claims.kb/standing.kb/standing-is-standing-according-to.md`
names for standing itself: one mark asked to carry two questions.

The live instance is `formalize/design.claims.kb/run.kb/what-the-stopping-rule-is.md`:
"the question presupposed one and dissolved once that
presupposition was named." Its `standing: user` is right -- the user
did rule on it -- but nothing today distinguishes that ruling from an
ordinary accepted claim. It wants its own mark, `dissolved: true`,
sibling to `standing:` and to `defeated:`, mutually exclusive with the
latter: a claim whose question was never well-formed was never live
enough to be defeated.
