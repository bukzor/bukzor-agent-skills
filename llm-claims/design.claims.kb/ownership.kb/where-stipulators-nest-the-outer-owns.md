# Where stipulators nest, the outer owns

Where two stipulators of one word nest, the outer one owns and the
inner entry is inert. The law without this rule read every inside
double as a contest instead of a restatement: a scanner built that
way reported six false errors on the current fleet, one per inside
double.

The declined alternative is innermost-wins, on the reading that a
word glossed differently at a finer grain should resolve to the finer
gloss. It mistakes what a stipulation carries: `ontology:` is a word
list, and ownership is exclusion, while the gloss lives in the
theory's prose and already resolves by proximity -- a claim inside a
theory is read with that theory's prose in view, whoever owns the
word. What innermost-wins would actually change is who is barred, and
it bars the inner theory's own siblings from a word the parent had
granted them.

It is unimplementable besides. Shadowing asks the checker to tell the
parent's broad sense from the child's narrow one, and they are the
same string; a sibling saying the word could not be judged either way.
A genuinely different sense takes `uniquify` -- its own string -- and
a genuine intent to narrow takes culling the outer entry and keeping
the inner, which says so explicitly. What would reopen this is a
notation that marks a sense-switch at a scope boundary, visible to a
reader and a scan alike. Every nested pair the fleet has produced has
been plain re-listing, never a narrowing.

The rule's commonest case is a ledger root, where it does something
the trespass scan cannot show. A root has no siblings, so REACH
gives it the whole ledger and its entry can never charge anyone; what
the entry still does is bar every theory below from fencing the word.
A root listing is the ledger declaring a word common property -- which
is what a word every theory says needs, and why promoting one there is
a repair and not an evasion.
