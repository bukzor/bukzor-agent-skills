# Words and labels are two sorts of one law

`ontology:` words and claim labels are two sorts under one supertype:
they share the licensing law and differ in exactly two semantic
parameters -- what an unowned name means (DEFAULT) and the shape of
the finding (TRESPASS vs DANGLING). Their apparent difference in
scope reduces to recognition: whether a name is a label at all is the
one fleet-wide fact about either sort.

Unification is a supertype, not an identification -- the sorts are
not the same object, and the parameters prove it: an unowned word is
a license, an unowned label is a defect. A third parameter once
claimed here, whether uniqueness is checked, turned out to be a fact
about the lint suite rather than about the language, and is demoted.
