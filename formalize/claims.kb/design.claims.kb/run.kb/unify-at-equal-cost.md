# Unify at Equal Cost

Where two notions can be unified at equal cost, unify them.

All else equal, one concept beats two: it halves what a reader carries,
and it is what makes a structure show up at all -- two notions kept
apart never reveal the law they both obey. The equal-cost clause is the
whole of the restraint. A unification that forces either side to state
itself awkwardly has moved the cost rather than removing it.
